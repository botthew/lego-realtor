# The DMV First-Time Buyer Blueprint
## Your Step-by-Step Roadmap to Homeownership

---

## Introduction

Buying a home is the biggest financial decision most people make. But it doesn't have to be overwhelming.

This guide breaks down the entire home-buying process into simple, actionable steps—so you know exactly what to expect, when to expect it, and how to nail every stage.

Whether you're a first-time buyer in Arlington, Silver Spring, Bethesda, or anywhere in the DMV, this blueprint will keep you on track.

## The Timeline: Your 90-Day Journey

Most first-time buyers go from "maybe I should buy" to "keys in hand" in 90-120 days. Here's the realistic timeline:

- **Weeks 1-2:** Get Pre-Approved
- **Weeks 3-6:** House Hunt & Make an Offer
- **Weeks 7-10:** Inspection, Appraisal, Underwriting
- **Weeks 11-13:** Final Walk-Through & Closing

Let's break it down.

---

## Phase 1: Pre-Approval (Weeks 1-2)

### What It Is
Pre-approval is NOT a promise to lend. It's a lender's preliminary assessment of how much they'll likely lend you based on:
- Your credit score
- Your income (last 2 years tax returns)
- Your debts (student loans, car payments, credit cards)
- Your down payment savings
- Your employment history

### What You Need
Before talking to a lender, gather:
1. **Last 2 years of tax returns** (personal + business if self-employed)
2. **Last 2 months of pay stubs**
3. **Bank statements** (showing down payment savings)
4. **List of debts** (student loans, credit cards, car loans)
5. **Photo ID**
6. **Explanation letter** (if you have a recent late payment or credit issue)

### The Pre-Approval Process
1. **Contact 2-3 lenders** (compare rates, not just one)
   - Traditional banks (Wells Fargo, Bank of America)
   - Credit unions (often have better rates)
   - Mortgage brokers (shop multiple lenders)
2. **Apply online or in person** (takes 30 minutes)
3. **Underwriter reviews** (2-3 business days)
4. **You get a pre-approval letter** showing your max loan amount

### Key Number to Know: Your Maximum Offer Price
The lender tells you a number. **Don't spend all of it.**

**Smart rule:** Aim for a payment that's no more than 25-28% of your gross monthly income.

**Example:**
- Gross monthly income: $6,000
- 28% = $1,680/month max payment
- That $1,680 includes: mortgage principal + interest + taxes + insurance

If your max pre-approval is higher, it means the lender thinks you can afford it. But they also want to make money off you. Your comfort level matters more.

### Common Mistakes
- ❌ Changing jobs before closing
- ❌ Making large purchases on credit (car loan, furniture)
- ❌ Missing payments on anything (even one late payment can kill your approval)
- ❌ Closing old credit card accounts (hurts your credit score)

**Stay boring.** No big financial moves until after closing.

## Phase 2: House Hunt & Make an Offer (Weeks 3-6)

### How to Start Looking
1. **Work with a real estate agent** (they're free—sellers pay commission)
   - Ask for references from friends
   - Interview 2-3 agents
   - Pick one who listens and knows your target neighborhoods
2. **Use online tools**
   - Zillow, Redfin, Trulia (for browsing)
   - Local MLS listing (ask your agent for access)
3. **Attend open houses** (even if you're not interested—see what's out there)

### Things to Look For (Beyond "Pretty")
- **Age of roof** (can be expensive to replace)
- **HVAC system** (furnace/AC—replace cost is $5K-15K)
- **Foundation cracks** (small = normal, large = red flag)
- **Electrical panel** (aluminum = outdated, copper = good)
- **Water stains** (on ceilings/walls = leaked roof or plumbing)
- **Smell** (musty = moisture/mold problem)
- **Are you comfortable here for 5+ years?**

### When You Find "The One"

**Step 1: Calculate Your Offer**
- Research comparable homes (comps) that sold in last 3 months
- Account for condition, location, square footage
- Ask your agent: "What should I offer?"

**Step 2: Submit Your Offer**
Your offer should include:
- **Offer price** (your bid)
- **Down payment %** (typically 5-20%)
- **Earnest money** ($500-2,000 deposit showing you're serious)
- **Financing contingency** ("offer valid if I get approved")
- **Inspection contingency** ("offer valid if inspection passes")
- **Appraisal contingency** ("offer valid if home appraises for offer price")
- **Closing date** (typically 45-60 days out)

**Step 3: Negotiate**
- Seller accepts, rejects, or counters your offer
- Back-and-forth until you agree (or walk away)
- Once agreed, you're **under contract**

### Tips for Competitive Offers
- Make your offer within 24 hours of seeing the home (serious buyer signal)
- Pre-approval letter attached to offer (shows you're qualified)
- Consider escalation clause ("I'll pay $5K more if you get a higher offer, up to $X")
- In hot markets (DC, Arlington): Consider short inspection period (7-10 days instead of 10-14)
- Written reason why seller should pick you (helps in close bids)

## Phase 3: Inspection, Appraisal & Underwriting (Weeks 7-10)

### The Inspection (Week 1 After Contract)

**What It Is**
A professional inspector walks the home for 2-3 hours and writes a detailed report about:
- Structural condition
- All systems (electrical, plumbing, HVAC)
- Roof condition
- Foundation
- Any repair needs

**Cost:** $300-500

**How to Choose an Inspector**
- Ask your agent for 2-3 recommendations
- Check reviews on Google/Yelp
- Make sure they're licensed in MD/VA/DC
- Ask: "Will you attend the inspection?" (good ones say yes)

**What Happens If You Find Problems**
You have 2 options:
1. **Renegotiate price** ("Fix this and lower price by $X")
2. **Seller makes repairs** (often poor quality)
3. **You fix it after purchase** (most common)

**Common Issues & Costs:**
- Roof replacement: $10K-20K
- HVAC replacement: $5K-15K
- Plumbing repair: $500-5K
- Electrical panel upgrade: $1K-3K
- Foundation repair: $3K-50K (varies wildly)

**Most inspections find minor stuff** (caulk the bathroom, paint trim, replace weatherstripping). Don't kill the deal over $500 repairs.

### The Appraisal (Week 2-3)

**What It Is**
The lender hires an appraiser to confirm the home is worth what you're paying for it.

Bank's logic: "If the buyer stops paying, we want to know the home is worth enough to sell at auction and recover our loan."

**Cost:** $400-600 (you pay, added to loan)

**Red Flag: Low Appraisal**
If home appraises below your offer price, you have 3 options:
1. **Pay the difference in cash**
2. **Renegotiate the price down**
3. **Walk away** (if you have appraisal contingency)

Example:
- You offered $500K
- Home appraises at $485K
- You either pay extra $15K cash, ask seller to lower to $485K, or walk

This is rare in the DMV, but happens in competitive markets.

### Underwriting (Week 2-3)

**What It Is**
The underwriter is the lender's quality control. They verify:
- Your income is real (calls your employer)
- Your credit is still good (no new large debts)
- Your down payment came from your savings (not a loan)
- All documents are legit

**What They'll Ask For:**
- Letter of employment (don't lose this job!)
- Updated bank statements
- Explanation of any new debt
- Your mortgage insurance quote (if down payment < 20%)

**How Long:** 2-3 weeks

**Red Flags That Kill Approval:**
- You changed jobs (especially switch careers)
- You bought a car on credit
- You opened new credit cards
- Large unexplained transfer of money into your account
- You became self-employed in the last 2 years

**Bottom Line:** Stay financially boring until you close.

### Timeline Reality Check
This phase takes 4 weeks minimum. Add 1 week for each issue found.

## Phase 4: Final Walk-Through & Closing (Weeks 11-13)

### The Final Walk-Through (Day Before Closing)

**What It Is**
You go to the home 24 hours before closing to verify:
- All agreed repairs are complete
- No new damage since inspection
- Agreed items are still there (light fixtures, appliances, etc.)
- Home is in agreed condition

**Bring Your Agent**—they'll help you verify everything.

**If You Find Issues:**
- Photos/video of the problem
- Message seller/agent immediately
- Decide: Fix before closing, escrow money for repair, or walk away

### Closing (The Final Day)

**What Happens**
1. You meet at closing attorney's office (or title company)
2. Attorney/title company walks you through all documents
3. You review closing disclosure (final loan/cost breakdown)
4. You sign ~100 pages (feels like a lot, but mostly repetitive)
5. You wire down payment + closing costs
6. Attorney records deed + mortgage with government
7. You get keys

**Time:** 1-2 hours

**What You're Signing**
- **Promissory note** = your promise to repay the loan
- **Mortgage document** = gives lender claim on house if you don't pay
- **Closing disclosure** = final accounting of all costs
- **Title docs** = proof of ownership transfer
- Various other docs (lender requirements, insurance stuff, etc.)

**Bring:**
- Photo ID
- Certified check or wire confirmation for down payment/closing costs
- Insurance binder (proof you have homeowners insurance)
- Your attorney (if getting separate one; usually title company provides)

### Closing Costs: What to Expect

Typical closing costs are 2-5% of loan amount.

**Breakdown:**
- Loan origination fee: 0.5-1%
- Appraisal: $400-600
- Title insurance: $500-1,500
- Attorney fees: $500-1,500
- Title search: $200-400
- Property taxes (prepaid): $1,000-3,000
- Homeowners insurance (prepaid): $500-2,000
- Mortgage insurance (if <20%): $1,000-5,000
- Inspection: $300-500
- Surveys/misc: $200-500

**Example for $500K purchase with 10% down:**
- Loan amount: $450K
- Closing costs: $9,000-22,500 (2-5%)
- Down payment: $50,000
- **Total cash needed: $59,000-72,500**

**Pro Tip:** Ask seller to cover some closing costs if market is competitive. Common: seller covers 1-3% of purchase price in buyer closing costs.

### After Closing

**Congratulations! You own a home.**

But now:
- Set up autopay for mortgage
- Change mailing address with USPS
- Update insurance, car registration, etc.
- Plan your first improvements
- Start budgeting for maintenance (roof, HVAC, plumbing will fail eventually)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Getting pre-approved by only one lender**
   - Shop at least 2-3. Rates vary 0.5-1%

2. **Stretching your budget to the max**
   - Just because they'll lend it doesn't mean you should borrow it
   - Keep 6 months emergency fund after closing
   - Account for maintenance, property taxes, insurance

3. **Making large purchases before closing**
   - Car loan, furniture credit, new job = approval killed

4. **Ignoring inspection problems**
   - Get multiple bids on major repairs
   - Don't skip inspection contingency to be competitive

5. **Not getting homeowners insurance pre-closing**
   - You can't close without proof of insurance
   - Shop rates 4-6 weeks before closing

6. **Choosing wrong agent**
   - Wrong agent = wrong strategy, missed homes, poor negotiation
   - Interview 2-3. Pick the one who listens.

7. **Skipping contingencies**
   - Inspection, appraisal, financing contingencies protect you
   - Only skip if you're all-cash or insanely confident
   - In tight markets, shorten timelines instead of removing protections

8. **Not understanding your loan type**
   - FHA (3.5% down, but mortgage insurance for life)
   - Conventional (5-20% down, mortgage insurance if <20%)
   - VA (0% down if eligible)
   - USDA (0% down if in rural area)
   - Get clear on which is best for you

## Your DMV Real Estate Market Context

### Why It Matters
The DMV market (DC, Maryland, Virginia) is competitive. Homes move fast.

**Typical timeline:**
- Hot markets: 5-10 days on market
- Slow markets: 20-45 days on market
- In competition, you may lose to all-cash buyers or higher bids

**Price ranges vary by area:**
- Arlington: $500K-$800K (condos/townhomes)
- Bethesda: $700K-$1.5M (suburban homes)
- Silver Spring: $400K-$700K (diverse inventory)
- DC proper: $450K-$1.2M (neighborhood dependent)

**Your competitive advantages:**
- Pre-approval letter (shows you're serious)
- Quick decision-making (respond fast)
- inspection contingency timing (shorter = more appealing)
- Pre-approved mortgage (not just pre-qualified)
